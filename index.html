Flagship · Live

DubDubDone

Dub any scene with your friends.

Load a clip, grab a character, and everyone performs their lines together in one shared pass — then take the finished video away. It runs entirely in the browser with nothing to install: drop in any MP4 and it drafts the script for you by finding the speech, or load ready-made voice packs. Game modes run from straight dubbing to Karaoke, Radio Theatre, Chaos, and Telephone.

Why it's interesting: the hard part of dubbing together isn't the dubbing — it's latency. Route voices through a call and every remote performer lands 100–300 ms late. DubDubDone's fix is architectural: voices never cross the network during a take. The server says "go," every player plays and records locally, and takes upload afterward aligned to the scene timeline — so someone on a terrible connection sounds identical to the host. Add in-browser MP4 export by remuxing (seconds, no re-encode), a Cloudflare Workers + D1 backend serving site and API on one origin, and 400-plus tests, and it's a serious engineering build dressed up as a party game.

Sel-Fi

A marketplace for seller-financed housing.

Sel-Fi connects property sellers and buyers for direct deals — seller-financed, rent-to-own, and private sales. Sellers keep their commission and earn interest; buyers get flexible paths to ownership outside the traditional mortgage gate. Browsable listings, buyer and investor profiles, and deal-structure detail pages.

Why it's interesting: it's domain knowledge turned into product. Creative financing is a real but underserved corner of housing — my urban planning and real estate finance background shaped the deal structures, the listing data model, and the buyer-profile matching idea.

azharmoolla.github.io
Zero-infrastructure · Open source

Home Finder

A full search-and-ranking app that costs $0 to run.

A live home-hunting tool — rentals and for-sale listings side by side — where a Google Sheet is the database, GitHub Pages is the host, and a small Apps Script endpoint handles write-back. Every listing is scored 0–100 by an honesty-first engine: graduated points for what a listing actually states, unknowns flagged "confirm this" instead of fake-penalized, and missing must-haves capping the score so a mystery listing can never look like a 95.

Why it's interesting: it's maximum product from minimum infrastructure, built to generalize. You pick up to three anchor places — work, school, family — and the map weighs every home against all of them; the rubric adapts when a listing is for sale instead of for rent; shared workflow fields track a team's outreach. No server, no hosting bill. I also wrote a public step-by-step guide so anyone can rebuild it for any search: cars, jobs, anything.
